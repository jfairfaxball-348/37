# Novelty / prior-art investigation

A compelling finite pattern is **not** a novel theorem. File an initial screen with *each* potentially interesting/useful lead, before investing in a full proof. N1 and N2 are stronger independent audits of the exact statement.

## Mandatory audit record
1. Identifier, date, **exact** proposition with quantifiers and hypotheses; distinguish observation, conjecture, proof and formal check.
2. Literal queries and variants: modular order, repunit indices, cyclotomic divisibility, order statistics, prime splitting, digit cycles, base dependence, group actions; plus notation and equivalent formulations appropriate to the candidate.
3. OEIS sequence/formula searches, reference textbooks and specialist literature, primary publications/preprints with date/DOI/arXiv when available, references and forward citations where accessible. Record URLs, access dates and whether **abstract**, **theorem text**, or **proof** was actually read.
4. Nearest 3–5 comparators: exact theorem statements, assumptions, implications and whether each *subsumes*, *overlaps* or *fails to cover* the proposed result. Explanations that merely restate known algebra are NOT_NOVEL.
5. Candidate significance: does it prove something not reducible to a known routine identity? Would a competent specialist care beyond a numerological curiosity?
6. Verdict `NOT_NOVEL`, `UNCERTAIN`, `CANDIDATE_DISTINCT` (never 'certified novel'), or `REFUTED`; unresolved missing sources, independent-review requirement and re-audit triggers.

A search with no result is **not evidence of priority**. Never invent bibliography, assert openness, quote an unseen proof or use AI agreement as an independent audit. N2 must audit the *proved result* rather than a conveniently broader conjecture.

## Foundational baseline — 2026-10-09
- `10²+10+1=111=3·37` and `ord_37(10)=3` are standard algebra and computational facts, not project discoveries.
- OEIS A172372, https://oeis.org/A172372 , explicitly studies least repunit lengths for primes (accessed 2026-10-09).
- Wolfram MathWorld, https://mathworld.wolfram.com/MultiplicativeOrder.html , gives the multiplicative-order/repetend relationship (accessed 2026-10-09).
- OEIS A122787, https://oeis.org/A122787 , specifically lists 37 in an order-of-10-power-of-three context (accessed 2026-10-09).

**This baseline is a limited orientation screen, not a completed deep N1 audit** and says nothing about future unpublished hypotheses.
