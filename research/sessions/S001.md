## Research observations: experimental data, not proof or novelty
`[2,2000]` contains 1999 bases per prime, 7996 attempted pairs. **7784** are coprime/included; **212** excluded. All 7784 were checked individually by a distinct algorithm using a direct multiplicative recurrence and the recurrence `R_{k+1}=bR_k+1 (mod p)`. Zero unexplained oracle mismatches. Per prime:

| p | p mod 3 | included | unique nonzero residues | order-three residues | primitive roots (unique) |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|---:|
| 31 | 1 | 1935 | 30 | 5,25 | 8 |
| 37 | 1 | 1945 | 36 | 10,26 | 12 |
| 41 | 2 | 1951 | 40 | none | 16 |
| 43 | 1 | 1953 | 42 | 6,36 | 12 |

For 37 there are 108 bases in the window with order three, but only two residues: each such residue recurs 54 times; these are **not 108 independent phenomena**. For 37, 649 bases had full order 36; exactly 12 residue classes are primitive roots, as `phi(36)=12`. The full order histogram is in the independently checked [manifest](../data/S001_manifest.json). The full **unfiltered** 1,685,822-byte JSON is preserved as deterministic [gzip archive](../data/S001_full.json.gz) (36,122 bytes). The uncompressed raw JSON is also in the GitHub Actions artifact. No cherry-picked windows, primes or bases.

For every p, every nonzero residue appears in the sweep. Order counts per distinct residue coincide with `phi(d)` for each `d | p-1`, i.e. the multiplicative group is cyclic. The order-three residues are the roots of `Phi_3(b)=b²+b+1` modulo p; exactly two when p≡1 mod 3, none when p≡2 mod 3. For b≡1 mod p, `ord_p(b)=1` but least repunit length is p; otherwise the lengths agree. Each statement is standard algebra, not evidence of originality.
