## Definitions and caution on local vs genus vs class
Form Q=[a,b,c] means ax²+bxy+cy², gcd(a,b,c)=1, a>0, discriminant Δ=b²−4ac<0, with Δ fundamental only where explicitly stated. **Primitive integer representation** adds gcd(x,y)=1, and local primitive representation requires x,y in Z_l with at least one l-adic unit and Q(x,y)=n for every finite l, plus positive real representation. Proper equivalence uses SL₂(Z); improper uses GL₂(Z) and identifies inverse proper classes when positive definite. The proper classes for maximal imaginary quadratic orders form Cl(Δ); genera are cosets of Cl(Δ)². **Local solubility controls genus, not a specified proper class**. The class-field Frobenius class distinguishes prime representations; a spinor-genus discussion for rank two (Estes–Pall) is NOT the same as ternary spinor-exception theory (Schulze-Pillot), nor may a ternary local-global theorem be transplanted to binary forms. Conductor f>1 uses ring class group, f-coprimality and ramification restrictions; none is silently folded into fundamental Δ. The more recent PID/hyperelliptic generalization of Dallaporta does not prove a missing special binary theorem.

## Mechanism Q1: can local primitive representation of a prime force a fixed class when the genus is unique?
**Exact quantified falsifiable test Q1**, NOT an endorsed conjecture: For every **negative fundamental** discriminant Δ<0 whose proper positive definite primitive form classes make exactly one genus, every primitive positive definite integer form Q of discriminant Δ and every positive prime p not dividing Δ, if Q primitively represents p over R and over Z_l for **every prime l**, does there exist (x,y) in Z² with gcd(x,y)=1 and Q(x,y)=p?

**Established negative illustration / countermodel (classical, not a project result):** Δ=−23, proper classes [1,1,6], [2,1,3], [2,−1,3], class group of order 3 and one genus. The nonprincipal form [2,1,3] primitively represents p=2; because all classes lie in one genus, the principal form [1,1,6] admits a locally primitive representation at every completion. Yet [1,1,6] does not integrally represent 2. This is an illustrative counterexample from the standard reduction/genus framework, NOT an S006-discovered theorem. Class group odd part distinguishes the classes despite one genus. A naive 'one genus means one class' implication is false. Do not extrapolate the claim to arbitrary f, nonsquarefree n or nonprimitive representations.

## Mechanism Q2: do prime-representation sets identify an individual positive definite form class?
**Exact quantified falsifiable test Q2**, not a project conjecture: For all negative **fundamental** discriminants Δ and all primitive positive definite integral binary forms F,G of discriminant Δ, suppose for **every rational prime p not dividing Δ**, there exist primitive integers x,y with F(x,y)=p if and only if there exist primitive integers u,v with G(u,v)=p. Must F and G then be **GL₂(Z)-equivalent** (not necessarily properly SL₂(Z)-equivalent)?

The restriction to GL₂ is crucial: [a,b,c] and [a,−b,c] have equal integer value sets by y↦−y but may represent different proper classes. Under the classical imaginary quadratic form/class field correspondence, Frobenius in the Hilbert class field distinguishes prime representation by the inverse pair {c,c⁻¹}; the plausible implication of Q2 is an **already-standard Chebotarev/class-field consequence**, not an original proof claim made here. Exact unramified prime and fundamental-discriminant quantifiers matter. We have **not** independently read a complete primary theorem proving this exact Q2 formulation; treat its proof comparison as BACKGROUND / NEEDS HUMAN CHECK, never declare a certified new gap.
